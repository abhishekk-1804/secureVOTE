## 1. Executive Summary & Status Matrix

SecureVOTE 3.1 introduces privacy-preserving zero-knowledge ballot-validity proofs for additive Exponential ElGamal voting over NIST P-256 (`secp256r1`). This milestone resolves the primary integrity vulnerability of SecureVOTE 3.0: the threat of malicious vote-stuffing via malformed ciphertexts.

However, in accordance with scientific rigor and academic integrity, **SecureVOTE 3.1 DOES NOT claim full End-to-End Verifiability (E2E-V)**. An election system achieves true E2E-V only when three independent properties are simultaneously satisfied:
1. **Cast as Intended**: The voter can verify that the ciphertext corresponds to their intended selection without compromising secrecy.
2. **Recorded as Cast**: The voter can verify that their encrypted ballot appears unchanged on the public bulletin board.
3. **Tallied as Recorded**: Anyone can verify that the published tally is the exact homomorphic sum of all valid recorded ballots.

SecureVOTE 3.1 demonstrates (2) and (3), ensuring under computational soundness in the random oracle model that tallied ballots satisfy $\sum v_j = 1$ and $v_j \in \{0, 1\}$. Property (1) remains an open research area.

## 2. Deep Dive: Architectural & Cryptographic Gaps

### 2.1 Formal Proof Gap vs Implementation Assurance

* **Current Implementation Assurance**:
  - The CDS94 disjunctive proof and Chaum-Pedersen equality proof algorithms strictly adhere to the mathematical specifications defined in Cramer, Damgård, and Schoenmakers (Crypto '94) and Chaum and Pedersen (Crypto '92).
  - Domain separation tags (`SECUREVOTE31/CDS94/DISJUNCTIVE`, `SECUREVOTE31/CHAUM_PEDERSEN/SUM_EQUALITY`) prevent cross-protocol challenge substitution.
  - 17 proof-specific unit tests and 12 verifier tests empirically validate detection of ciphertext point tampering, proof scalar tampering, challenge tampering, candidate index mismatch, non-binary witness injection, replay attacks across elections, altered domain separators, and mixed-proof packages.
* **The Gap**:
  - Implementation assurance is empirical, not formal. There is no machine-checked formal verification (using Coq, EasyCrypt, or CryptoVerif) proving that the Python implementation is free of side-channel timing leaks or subtle group subgroup attacks (though secp256r1 has cofactor $h=1$, eliminating small-subgroup attacks).
  - Software engineers must recognize that "passes test suite" $\neq$ "formally proven bug-free".

### 2.2 Single-Authority Decryption & Missing DKG Roadmap (Milestone 3.2)

* **Current Limitation**:
  - The election private key scalar $x \in \mathbb{Z}_q^*$ is generated by a central authority. If the private key is compromised, an adversary can decrypt all historical ballots and violate voter privacy retroactively.
* **Planned Resolution (Milestone 3.2: Threshold ElGamal & Pedersen DKG)**:
  1. **Trustee Slate**: $n$ independent election trustees (e.g., representatives from rival political parties and civil society).
  2. **Pedersen $(t, n)$ Distributed Key Generation**: Trustees jointly compute election public key $Y = \sum_{i=1}^n Y_i$ without any single party ever knowing $x$.
  3. **Verifiable Decryption Shares**: For the aggregate tally $(A, B)$, each trustee $i$ publishes share $S_i = x_i \cdot A$ accompanied by a non-interactive Chaum-Pedersen proof of discrete log equality:
     $$\text{PoK}\{(x_i) : Y_i = x_i \cdot G \wedge S_i = x_i \cdot A\}$$
  4. **Lagrange Reconstruction**: Any quorum of $t$ honest trustees can reconstruct $x \cdot A = \sum_{j \in \mathcal{T}} \lambda_j S_j$, allowing decryption of the final election tally while keeping individual ballots permanently secret.

### 2.3 Mix-Nets & Anonymity Gaps

* **Current Limitation**:
  - Homomorphic tallying eliminates the need to decrypt individual ballots, which provides strong secrecy for the final result.
  - However, in small precincts (e.g., < 20 voters) or elections where all voters choose the same candidate, homomorphic tallying reveals how individuals voted.
  - Furthermore, if intermediate tallies are observed, or if the order of ballots on the public ledger matches the physical arrival order of voters at the polling booth, voter privacy is deanonymized via temporal correlation.
* **Planned Resolution**:
  - Implementation of verifiable re-encryption shuffles (e.g., Bayer-Groth or Wikström mix-nets) for scenarios requiring individual ballot inspection or precinct-level anonymization.
  - Cryptographic batching and shuffling before ledger commitment.

### 2.4 Lack of Individual Cast-as-Intended Verification (Benaloh Challenge)

* **Current Limitation**:
  - In SecureVOTE 3.1, the voting terminal (or voter client) generates randomness $r_j$, computes ciphertexts $(C_{1,j}, C_{2,j})$, and generates ZK proofs $\pi_j$ and $\pi_{\text{sum}}$.
  - If the voting terminal firmware is compromised (e.g., infected by malware), it could secretly cast a vote for Candidate B while showing Candidate A on the screen, generating a valid ZK proof for Candidate B.
* **Planned Resolution (Benaloh Audit Protocol)**:
  1. Terminal prepares encrypted ballot $C$ and validity proof $\pi$.
  2. Terminal displays commitment to the voter.
  3. Voter's independent verification device (e.g., mobile smartphone or auditor terminal) presents an interactive challenge: **"Audit" or "Cast"**.
  4. If "Audit" is chosen: The terminal must disclose the nonces $r_j$. The auditor device checks that $C$ genuinely encrypts Candidate A. The audited ballot is spoiled and never tallied.
  5. If "Cast" is chosen: The ballot is committed to the digital ballot box and tallied.
  6. By randomly auditing a statistical fraction of ballots, rogue terminal software is detected with exponential certainty.

### 2.5 Empirical Benchmark & Multi-Trial Overhead Analysis

#### Resolution of 100-Ballot Overhead Investigation
Earlier unisolated single-run profiling observed anomalous spikes (~199s) due to active `tracemalloc` instrumentation accumulating Python memory tracking frames across sequential loops. With explicit garbage collection (`gc.collect()`) prior to each trial and 3 independent trials per scale, generation overhead scales strictly linearly at ~0.82 seconds per ballot (~82.3s total for 100 ballots), consistent with the theoretical ~4.9x per-ballot scalar multiplication workload.

### 2.6 Verifier Operation Count & Complexity Breakdown

Full verification of ballot validity executes:
$$\text{Scalar Multiplications per Ballot} = 8k + 4$$
where $k$ is the number of candidates.
For a standard 4-candidate ballot ($k=4$), this requires exactly $8(4) + 4 = 36$ curve scalar multiplications on `secp256r1`:
- 8 scalar multiplications per candidate slot ($2 \times 2$ for Branch 0 equations, $2 \times 2$ for Branch 1 equations)
- 4 scalar multiplications for the aggregate Chaum-Pedersen sum equality proof ($2$ for equation 1, $2$ for equation 2)

### 2.7 Verifier Status Semantics

The standalone verifier enforces strict tripartite semantics:
1. `VALID`: Proofs are structurally well-formed and satisfy all $8k+4$ algebraic equations.
2. `INVALID`: Proof equations fail, challenges mismatch, points are not on curve, or a mixed-proof package is submitted.
3. `NOT_PRESENT`: Proofs are missing. In `SECUREVOTE31` mode, this causes the checkpoint to fail (`PASSED = False`). In legacy `SECUREVOTE3` mode, this is recorded as `NOT_VERIFIED`.

## 3. Engineering Guidance for Contributors

When contributing to SecureVOTE:
1. **Never use the phrase "provably unhackable" or "100% secure".**
2. **Always state assumptions**: ElGamal privacy relies on the Decisional Diffie-Hellman (DDH) assumption on NIST P-256.
3. **Always document the trust perimeter**: Clearly distinguish between client-side security, transit security, ledger integrity, and verifier independence.
4. **Maintain strict test coverage**: Any modification to cryptographic primitives must include mutation tests, edge-case checks, and standalone verifier validation.
