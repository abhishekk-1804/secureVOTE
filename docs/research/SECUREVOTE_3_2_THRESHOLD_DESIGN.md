# SecureVOTE 3.2 — Verifiable Threshold Decryption Architecture & Protocol Design

- **Milestone Target**: `v3.2.0-research`
- **Baseline Protocol**: `v3.1.0-research` (Frozen at commit `812aa9d`)
- **Status**: Comprehensive Architectural & Cryptographic Design Specification (Pre-Implementation Revision)
- **Configuration Target**: $(t, n) = (2, 3)$ Verifiable Threshold ElGamal

---

## 1. Executive Summary & Research Motivation

In SecureVOTE 3.0 and 3.1, election privacy relies on a single central private key scalar $x \in \mathbb{Z}_q^*$. While homomorphic aggregation prevents individual ballots from being decrypted during the tallying process, holding the private key in a single location introduces structural vulnerabilities:
1. **Single Point of Compromise (SPOF)**: An adversary who compromises the central server or extracts $x$ from memory can retroactively decrypt every historical ballot cast by every voter.
2. **Single Point of Failure**: Accidental loss or corruption of the private key renders the entire election tally permanently indecipherable.
3. **Absence of Institutional Separation of Powers**: Democratic legitimacy requires that no single entity—neither the election authority, the infrastructure provider, nor an individual system administrator—possesses unilateral capability to decrypt votes.

SecureVOTE 3.2 addresses this structural limitation by replacing single-authority decryption with **Verifiable Threshold ElGamal Decryption**. Under this scheme:
- A joint election public key $Y$ is cooperatively generated by $n = 3$ independent election trustees.
- The corresponding private key $x$ never exists in any single location, at any point in time.
- Any quorum of $t = 2$ honest trustees can cooperatively decrypt the aggregate election tally.
- Any coalition of fewer than $t = 2$ trustees learns no information regarding the shared secret beyond public parameters, under the stated model.
- Every trustee's partial decryption is accompanied by a non-interactive zero-knowledge proof of correctness, enabling public detection and rejection of malicious shares.

## 2. Threat Model & Operational Assumptions

### 2.1 Principals & Roles
1. **Trustees ($\mathcal{T}_1, \dots, \mathcal{T}_n$)**: $n = 3$ independent institutional participants (e.g., Election Commission, Judicial Observer, Technical Auditor).
2. **Public Ledger / Bulletin Board**: Append-only broadcast medium storing public parameters, commitments, encrypted ballots, and trustee artifacts.
3. **Voting Terminals / Clients**: Edge devices that generate Exponential ElGamal ciphertexts under joint public key $Y$ with zero-knowledge ballot validity proofs.
4. **Standalone Verifier**: Offline independent auditor that validates all cryptographic artifacts and tallies.

### 2.2 Adversary Model & Bounds
- **Corrupted Trustees**: We assume a **static adversary** who corrupts at most $f \le t - 1 = 1$ of the $n = 3$ trustees prior to protocol initialization. Security against an adaptive adversary is explicitly out of scope.
- **Adversary Behavior**: Corrupted trustees may be Byzantine (actively deviate from protocol rules, submit invalid shares, abort, or collude).
- **Communication Network**:
  - **Broadcast Channel**: Authenticated broadcast channel (bulletin board) where all participants observe identical public transcripts.
  - **Point-to-Point Channels**: Authenticated, confidential pairwise communication (TLS / ECIES) between trustees.
  - **Synchrony**: Synchronous communication rounds during Distributed Key Generation (DKG).

### 2.3 Cryptographic Assumptions
1. **Decisional Diffie-Hellman (DDH)** on NIST P-256 (`secp256r1`): Semantic secrecy of encrypted ballots.
2. **Discrete Logarithm (DLOG)** on NIST P-256: Unforgeability of public verification keys and secret share recovery.
3. **Random Oracle Model (ROM)**: Simulation soundness of Fiat-Shamir transformed proofs using SHA-256.

---

## 3. Security Goals

1. **Threshold Secrecy ($t$-of-$n$ Secrecy)**:
   Any coalition of strictly fewer than $t = 2$ trustees learns no information about unaggregated plaintext votes beyond public election outcomes, under the stated model.
2. **Correctness**:
   If at least $t = 2$ qualified trustees submit valid partial decryptions, the reconstructed tally is mathematically guaranteed to equal the exact sum of all valid cast votes.
3. **Robustness & Liveness**:
   The tally can be successfully decrypted and verified despite the failure, disconnection, or malicious behavior of up to $n - t = 1$ trustee.
4. **Malicious-Share Detection**:
   Any malformed or altered partial decryption share is publicly detectable via non-interactive zero-knowledge verification, preventing corrupted trustees from altering the election tally.
5. **Universal Verifiability of Tally**:
   Any third party can independently verify every stage: DKG key generation, ballot validity, homomorphic aggregation, partial decryption share proofs, and Lagrange combination.

---

## 4. Non-Goals & Explicit Scope Boundaries

SecureVOTE 3.2 explicitly **DOES NOT claim**:
1. **Full End-to-End Verifiability (E2E-V)**: Cast-as-intended verification (Benaloh challenge / spoiled-ballot audit) is deferred to future work.
2. **Coercion Resistance or Receipt-Freeness**: Does not defend against voters voluntarily or involuntarily disclosing encryption randomness.
3. **Mix-Net / Timing Anonymity**: Does not provide cryptographic shuffling; timing and order of ballots on the ledger remain potential side-channel vectors in small precincts.
4. **Adaptive-Adversary Security**: Does not defend against an adversary who dynamically corrupts trustees during or after the election lifecycle.
5. **Post-Quantum Security**: Vulnerable to Shor's algorithm on quantum architectures; relies entirely on classical elliptic curve hardness.
6. **Production Election Readiness**: Academic research prototype for applied cryptographic evaluation.

## 5. 2-of-3 System Model & Separation of Concerns

We establish consistent notation throughout the specification:
- **Total Trustees**: $n = 3$
- **Decryption Threshold**: $t = 2$
- **Corruption Bound**: $f \le t - 1 = 1$

```
+------------------------------------------------------------------------------------+
|                                PHASE 1: DKG                                       |
|  Trustees (T1, T2, T3) execute GJKR DKG:                                          |
|  - Commit to random polynomials (Pedersen VSS)                                     |
|  - Distribute and verify pairwise shares; resolve complaints -> Qualified Set QUAL |
|  - Reveal Feldman commitments -> Compute Joint Public Key Y and Verification Keys |
+------------------------------------------------------------------------------------+
                                         |
                                         v
+------------------------------------------------------------------------------------+
|                 PHASE 2: VOTING & HOMOMORPHIC AGGREGATION                          |
|  Voters encrypt choices under joint public key Y:                                  |
|  - Slot CDS94 disjunctive proofs (v_j in {0,1})                                    |
|  - Chaum-Pedersen sum equality proof (sum v_j = 1)                                 |
|  - Aggregator computes homomorphic sum: (A_j, B_j) = sum(Enc_Y(v_j))               |
+------------------------------------------------------------------------------------+
                                         |
                                         v
+------------------------------------------------------------------------------------+
|                  PHASE 3: VERIFIABLE THRESHOLD DECRYPTION                          |
|  Any t = 2 trustees (e.g., T1, T2) compute:                                        |
|  - Partial decryption shares: W_{i,j} = x_i * A_j                                  |
|  - Chaum-Pedersen equality proofs: log_G(Y_i) == log_A(W_{i,j})                   |
|  - Verifier validates proofs -> Reconstructs x * A_j via Lagrange interpolation    |
|  - Recovers tally T_j = DLog_G(B_j - x * A_j)                                      |
+------------------------------------------------------------------------------------+
```

### Separation of Core Properties:
- **Secrecy**: Ensured by Shamir's $(t, n)$ secret sharing ($t = 2$ required to reconstruct $x$).
- **Correctness**: Ensured by homomorphic Exponential ElGamal and discrete logarithm recovery.
- **Robustness / Liveness**: Ensured because any $t = 2$ out of $n = 3$ trustees suffice to reconstruct.
- **Malicious-Share Detection**: Ensured by non-interactive Chaum-Pedersen proofs published with each share.

## 6. Literature Foundations & Established Cryptographic Constructions

SecureVOTE 3.2 instantiates established, peer-reviewed cryptographic protocols:

1. **Threshold ElGamal Framework**:
   - *Cramer, Gennaro, and Schoenmakers* (EUROCRYPT 1997): Multi-authority threshold election architecture utilizing additive homomorphic encryption and distributed decryption.
2. **Verifiable Secret Sharing (VSS)**:
   - *Feldman* (FOCS 1987): Non-interactive verifiable secret sharing with public polynomial commitments in the exponent ($C_k = a_k \cdot G$).
   - *Pedersen* (CRYPTO 1991): Information-theoretic verifiable secret sharing using dual generators $(G, H)$ where $\log_G(H)$ is unknown, preventing share reconstruction from public commitments.
3. **Secure Distributed Key Generation (DKG)**:
   - *Gennaro, Jarecki, Krawczyk, and Rabin (GJKR)* (EUROCRYPT 1999 / Journal of Cryptology 2007): Formalized the security of discrete-log DKG protocols. Proved that plain Joint-Feldman DKG allows a malicious participant to bias the distribution of the public key, and provided the two-phase Pedersen-to-Feldman framework to guarantee a uniformly distributed joint secret key.
4. **Decryption Share Correctness Proof**:
   - *Chaum and Pedersen* (CRYPTO 1992): Zero-knowledge proof of equality of discrete logarithms proving $\log_G(Y_i) = \log_A(W_i)$.
5. **Non-Interactive Fiat-Shamir Transformation**:
   - *Fiat and Shamir* (CRYPTO 1986) with domain-separated hashing as formalized by *Bellare and Rogaway* (CCS 1993).

## 7. Distributed Key Generation (DKG) Specification

SecureVOTE 3.2 implements the **Gennaro-Jarecki-Krawczyk-Rabin (GJKR)** DKG framework adapted for NIST P-256 (`secp256r1`).

### 7.1 Protocol Structure & Execution Rounds

#### Phase 1: Commitment & Fixing (Pedersen VSS)
1. **Independent Generator**: Public parameters include generator $G$ and independent generator $H \in E(\mathbb{F}_p)$ derived via transparent hash-to-curve where $\log_G(H)$ is unknown.
2. **Polynomial Sampling**: Each trustee $i \in \{1, 2, 3\}$ samples two independent degree-$(t-1) = 1$ polynomials:
   $$f_i(z) = a_{i,0} + a_{i,1} \cdot z \pmod q$$
   $$f_i'(z) = b_{i,0} + b_{i,1} \cdot z \pmod q$$
   where $a_{i,0}, a_{i,1}, b_{i,0}, b_{i,1} \leftarrow_{\$} \mathbb{Z}_q^*$.
3. **Pedersen Commitment Broadcast**: Trustee $i$ publishes commitments:
   $$C_{i,k} = a_{i,k} \cdot G + b_{i,k} \cdot H \quad \text{for } k \in \{0, 1\}$$
   along with a Schnorr Proof of Knowledge on $a_{i,0}$ bound to $i$ and `election_id`.

#### Phase 2: Share Distribution
1. Trustee $i$ privately computes evaluations for trustee $j \in \{1, 2, 3\}$:
   $$s_{i \to j} = f_i(j) \pmod q, \quad s'_{i \to j} = f_i'(j) \pmod q$$
2. Trustee $i$ transmits $(s_{i \to j}, s'_{i \to j})$ to trustee $j$ over an encrypted and authenticated channel.

#### Phase 3: Share Verification & Complaint Handling
1. Upon receiving $(s_{i \to j}, s'_{i \to j})$, trustee $j$ verifies consistency against trustee $i$'s Pedersen commitments:
   $$s_{i \to j} \cdot G + s'_{i \to j} \cdot H \stackrel{?}{=} C_{i,0} + j \cdot C_{i,1}$$
2. **Complaint Broadcast**: If verification fails, trustee $j$ broadcasts a signed complaint `COMPLAINT(from=j, against=i)` to the ledger.
3. **Complaint Resolution**: If trustee $i$ receives a complaint from trustee $j$, trustee $i$ must broadcast $(s_{i \to j}, s'_{i \to j})$ publicly.
   - If the revealed share satisfies the public commitment, the complaint is dismissed.
   - If the revealed share fails or trustee $i$ fails to respond, trustee $i$ is disqualified.

#### Phase 4: Qualified Participant Set ($\mathcal{QUAL}$)
The set of qualified trustees $\mathcal{QUAL} \subseteq \{\mathcal{T}_1, \mathcal{T}_2, \mathcal{T}_3\}$ consists of all non-disqualified trustees. For $(t=2, n=3)$, protocol proceeds if $|\mathcal{QUAL}| \ge 2$. If fewer than 2 trustees qualify, the DKG aborts.

#### Phase 5: Feldman Extraction & Public Key Derivation
1. Each qualified trustee $i \in \mathcal{QUAL}$ reveals their Feldman commitments:
   $$A_{i,k} = a_{i,k} \cdot G \quad \text{for } k \in \{0, 1\}$$
2. All participants verify that $C_{i,k} == A_{i,k} + b_{i,k} \cdot H$.
3. **Joint Public Key $Y$**:
   $$Y = \sum_{i \in \mathcal{QUAL}} A_{i,0} = \left(\sum_{i \in \mathcal{QUAL}} a_{i,0}\right) \cdot G = x \cdot G$$
4. **Trustee Verification Keys $Y_j$**:
   For each trustee $j$, the public verification key is:
   $$Y_j = \sum_{i \in \mathcal{QUAL}} \left( A_{i,0} + j \cdot A_{i,1} \right) = x_j \cdot G$$

#### Phase 6: Final Share Computation
Each trustee $j \in \mathcal{QUAL}$ computes their final master private key share:
$$x_j = \sum_{i \in \mathcal{QUAL}} s_{i \to j} \pmod q$$
Notice that $x_j \cdot G == Y_j$. The master secret $x = \sum_{i \in \mathcal{QUAL}} a_{i,0} \bmod q$ is never reconstructed.

### 7.2 Purpose of Schnorr Proof on Constant Term
The Schnorr proof of knowledge of $a_{i,0}$ binds the secret contribution to trustee $i$'s authenticated identity, preventing key-cancellation / rogue-key attacks ($C_{i,0} = Y^* - \sum_{k \neq i} C_{k,0}$). **It does not by itself guarantee overall DKG security**; the full GJKR framework (Pedersen-then-Feldman verification, complaints, and qualified sets) is required to ensure uniform distribution of $x$ and robustness against malicious sharing.

## 10. Partial Decryption Protocol

Let $\mathbf{C} = (A_j, B_j)_{j=0}^{k-1}$ be the homomorphically aggregated tally ciphertext vector for candidate slots $j \in \{0, \dots, k-1\}$:
$$A_j = \sum_{\ell=1}^N C_{1,j}^{(\ell)} = R_j \cdot G$$
$$B_j = \sum_{\ell=1}^N C_{2,j}^{(\ell)} = R_j \cdot Y + T_j \cdot G$$
where $R_j$ is aggregate randomness and $T_j$ is the total vote count for candidate $j$.

Each participating trustee $i \in \mathcal{S}$ computes a **partial decryption share** for each candidate slot $j$:
$$W_{i,j} = x_i \cdot A_j$$
Notice that:
$$W_{i,j} = x_i \cdot (R_j \cdot G) = R_j \cdot (x_i \cdot G) = R_j \cdot Y_i$$

---

## 11. Decryption-Share Proof of Correctness

Trustee $i$ must prove knowledge of secret share $x_i$ satisfying:
$$\log_G(Y_i) = \log_{A_j}(W_{i,j}) = x_i$$
without disclosing $x_i$.

We instantiate the non-interactive **Chaum-Pedersen Discrete-Log Equality Proof**:

### Prover Algorithm (`prove_partial_decryption`)
1. Sample random blinding scalar $w \leftarrow_{\$} [1, q - 1]$.
2. Compute commitments:
   $$comm_1 = w \cdot G$$
   $$comm_2 = w \cdot A_j$$
3. Derive Fiat-Shamir challenge $c \in [1, q - 1]$ using canonical transcript:
   $$\text{domain} = \text{"SECUREVOTE32/ZKP/PARTIAL\_DECRYPT/"} \parallel \text{election\_id} \parallel \text{"/"} \parallel \text{candidate\_id} \parallel \text{"/TRUSTEE/"} \parallel i$$
   $$\text{transcript} = [G, Y_i, A_j, W_{i,j}, comm_1, comm_2]$$
   $$c = \mathcal{H}(\text{domain} \parallel \text{transcript}) \bmod (q - 1) + 1$$
4. Compute response scalar:
   $$s = (w + c \cdot x_i) \bmod q$$
5. Output proof tuple: $\Pi_{i,j} = (comm_1, comm_2, c, s)$.

---

## 12. Share Verification Protocol

Any observer or standalone verifier validates trustee $i$'s share $W_{i,j}$ using public parameters $(G, Y_i, A_j, W_{i,j})$ and proof $\Pi_{i,j} = (comm_1, comm_2, c, s)$:

### Verifier Algorithm (`verify_partial_decryption`)
1. **Point Validation**: Verify $W_{i,j}, comm_1, comm_2 \in E(\mathbb{F}_p)$ and none equal $\mathcal{O}$.
2. **Challenge Reconstruction**: Recompute challenge $c'$ from canonical transcript. Verify $c == c'$.
3. **Equation 1**:
   $$s \cdot G \stackrel{?}{=} comm_1 + c \cdot Y_i$$
4. **Equation 2**:
   $$s \cdot A_j \stackrel{?}{=} comm_2 + c \cdot W_{i,j}$$

*Cryptographic Assurance*: Successful verification establishes acceptance of the stated Chaum-Pedersen relation under the Random Oracle Model and discrete logarithm assumptions on secp256r1.

## 13. Lagrange Interpolation & Full Tally Reconstruction

For any valid quorum $\mathcal{S} \subset \mathcal{QUAL}$ with $|\mathcal{S}| = t = 2$:

### Step 1: Compute Lagrange Basis Coefficients
The Lagrange interpolation basis polynomial evaluated at $z = 0$ for trustee $i \in \mathcal{S}$ is:
$$\lambda_{i, \mathcal{S}} = \prod_{m \in \mathcal{S}, m \neq i} \frac{0 - m}{i - m} = \frac{-m}{i - m} \pmod q$$

#### Explicit Pre-Computed Coefficients for $(2, 3)$ Quorums:
- For $\mathcal{S} = \{1, 2\}$:
  $$\lambda_{1, \{1,2\}} = 2 \pmod q, \quad \lambda_{2, \{1,2\}} = -1 \equiv q - 1 \pmod q$$
- For $\mathcal{S} = \{2, 3\}$:
  $$\lambda_{2, \{2,3\}} = 3 \pmod q, \quad \lambda_{3, \{2,3\}} = -2 \equiv q - 2 \pmod q$$
- For $\mathcal{S} = \{1, 3\}$:
  $$\lambda_{1, \{1,3\}} = 3 \cdot 2^{-1} \pmod q, \quad \lambda_{3, \{1,3\}} = -2^{-1} \pmod q$$

### Step 2: Combine Partial Decryptions
The aggregate blinding factor $x \cdot A_j$ is reconstructed on the curve:
$$W_{\text{full}, j} = \sum_{i \in \mathcal{S}} \lambda_{i, \mathcal{S}} \cdot W_{i,j} = \left(\sum_{i \in \mathcal{S}} \lambda_{i, \mathcal{S}} x_i\right) \cdot A_j = x \cdot A_j$$

### Step 3: Extract Plaintext Tally
Subtract $W_{\text{full}, j}$ from ciphertext component $B_j$:
$$M_j = B_j - W_{\text{full}, j} = (R_j \cdot Y + T_j \cdot G) - (R_j \cdot x \cdot G) = T_j \cdot G$$

Solve for integer vote count $T_j$ using baby-step/giant-step in range $[0, M_{\text{max}}]$.

## 14. Fault Tolerance, Malicious Shares & Replay Defense

### 14.1 Missing Trustees (Crash / Denial-of-Service)
If $n - t = 1$ trustee is offline, the coordinator forms quorum $\mathcal{S}$ with the remaining 2 trustees. Decryption proceeds without liveness interruption.

### 14.2 Malicious Shares
If a corrupted trustee submits a tampered point $W_{i,j}' \neq x_i \cdot A_j$, Equation 2 of the Chaum-Pedersen proof fails. The share is rejected, and the coordinator falls back to the third trustee.

### 14.3 Duplicate Submissions
If multiple partial decryptions are received for the same trustee, the coordinator verifies the share against $Y_i$. If multiple valid shares arrive, only the first verified share is retained.

### 14.4 Domain Separation & Replay Defense
All transcripts embed:
- `election_id`
- `candidate_id`
- `trustee_id`
Replaying a share or proof across different election IDs, candidates, or trustee roles causes the Fiat-Shamir challenge to mismatch under the ROM assumption.

### 14.5 Modular Reduction Bias Limitation
The derivation $c = \text{SHA-256}(\cdot) \bmod (q - 1) + 1$ has a known minor non-uniform bias ($\approx 2^{-32}$) due to $2^{256} > q$. This is a documented research prototype limitation. A production system should employ wide-reduction (e.g., SHA-512 reduced mod $q$) or RFC 9380 Hash-to-Scalar.

| Protocol Phase | Operation | Mathematical Formula | Theoretical Scalar Mults ($k = 4$ candidates) |
| :--- | :--- | :---: | :---: |
| **DKG Phase 1** | Pedersen Commitments (per trustee) | $2 \times (a \cdot G + b \cdot H)$ | 4 scalar mults |
| **DKG Phase 3** | Share Verification (per peer share) | $s \cdot G + s' \cdot H == C_0 + j C_1$ | 3 scalar mults / share |

*Analysis*: While ballot validity checking scales linearly with ballots ($36N$ scalar mults for $N$ ballots), verifiable threshold decryption requires exactly **40 scalar multiplications total**, independent of the number of voters.

## Appendix C: Exact Cryptographic Equations

1. **Pedersen Polynomial Evaluations**:
   $$s_{i \to j} = f_i(j) = a_{i,0} + a_{i,1} \cdot j \pmod q$$
   $$s'_{i \to j} = f_i'(j) = b_{i,0} + b_{i,1} \cdot j \pmod q$$
2. **Pedersen Commitment Verification**:
   $$s_{i \to j} \cdot G + s'_{i \to j} \cdot H \stackrel{?}{=} C_{i,0} + j \cdot C_{i,1}$$
3. **Feldman Commitments**:
   $$A_{i,k} = a_{i,k} \cdot G$$
4. **Joint Public Key**:
   $$Y = \sum_{i \in \mathcal{QUAL}} A_{i,0} \pmod E$$
5. **Trustee Verification Key**:
   $$Y_j = \sum_{i \in \mathcal{QUAL}} \left( A_{i,0} + j \cdot A_{i,1} \right) = x_j \cdot G$$
6. **Partial Decryption Share**:
   $$W_{i,j} = x_i \cdot A_j$$
7. **Chaum-Pedersen Verification Equations**:
   $$s \cdot G = comm_1 + c \cdot Y_i$$
   $$s \cdot A_j = comm_2 + c \cdot W_{i,j}$$
8. **Lagrange Basis Coefficient ($z=0$)**:
   $$\lambda_{i, \mathcal{S}} = \prod_{m \in \mathcal{S}, m \neq i} \frac{-m}{i - m} \pmod q$$
9. **Blinding Factor Reconstruction**:
   $$W_{\text{full}, j} = \sum_{i \in \mathcal{S}} \lambda_{i, \mathcal{S}} \cdot W_{i,j} = x \cdot A_j$$
10. **Tally Group Element**:
    $$M_j = B_j - W_{\text{full}, j} = T_j \cdot G$$

## Appendix F: Implementation & Architectural Risks

1. **Pedersen Generator Generation**:
   $H$ must be derived deterministically via hash-to-curve with a domain separation tag (`SECUREVOTE32/PEDERSEN_H`) so that no party knows $\log_G(H)$.
2. **Side-Channel Timing in Private Share Operations**:
   Trustee partial decryption $W_{i,j} = x_i \cdot A_j$ uses private scalar $x_i$. In Python, big-integer multiplication is not constant-time. Acceptable for research prototype; documented as limitation.
3. **Threshold Scale Limit**:
   Threshold decryption recovers $T_j \cdot G$. Integer recovery relies on baby-step/giant-step, bounded by $M_{\text{max}} = 10,000$ for research evaluation.
